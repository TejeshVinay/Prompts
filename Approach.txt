# SCP Intelligent Alert Reduction & Investigation System

## Master Discovery, Feasibility Analysis & Solution Design Prompt

You are acting as a **Senior AI/ML Architect and Financial Surveillance Systems Architect**.

Your task is **NOT to immediately implement a solution**.

Your first responsibility is to deeply understand the existing SCP alert-generation ecosystem, the available datasets, the existing rule logic, the existing solution approach, and the business constraints.

Only after building that understanding should you determine whether the previously discussed AI/ML/Agentic approaches are feasible, which approaches are appropriate, and how the problem should ultimately be solved.

---

# 1. Business Problem

We are working with a financial-trade surveillance / Supervisory Control Portal (SCP) use case.

The current high-level flow is understood to be approximately:

```text
Upstream Trading Systems
        ↓
Data Ingestion / Processing
        ↓
Existing Deterministic Rules
        ↓
SCP Alerts
        ↓
Supervisor Review
        ↓
Signoff / RFI / Other Action
```

The existing deterministic rule engine is already operational and is considered by business stakeholders to be functioning reasonably well.

However, it generates a significant number of alerts that require manual supervisory review.

The approximate historical dataset available to us contains around **50,000 generated alerts** with a large number of attributes.

The business objective is to investigate whether AI/ML and agentic techniques can:

* reduce unnecessary formal alert generation where appropriate,
* reduce BAU / low-value alert volume,
* reduce supervisory investigation effort,
* identify genuinely unusual behaviour,
* provide contextual evidence around rule hits,
* improve alert prioritization and actionability,
* connect related alerts/trades/entities,
* and provide explainable reasoning for why an alert should or should not receive further attention.

There is an important business/control constraint:

> Once an official SCP alert is generated, the supervisor is expected to review it.

Therefore, **do not assume that an AI system is allowed to suppress or close an official alert**.

Instead, determine from the actual system architecture whether there is a meaningful intermediate concept such as:

```text
Rule Evaluation
      ↓
Rule Hit / Candidate
      ↓
Alert Creation
```

If such a candidate stage exists, investigate whether an intelligence layer could safely operate there.

If it does not exist, explicitly identify that limitation.
